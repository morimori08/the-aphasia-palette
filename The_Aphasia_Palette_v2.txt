The Aphasia Palette
By Vocadiablo (Yoshitaka Morita)


Act I — The Adjective Fog


Chapter 1　The Thesaurus on Fire

Episode 01: The Salt in the Mouth

[M1-C1-E01-S001]
　November rain needles the warehouse pane above Wapping Wall. On my plate lies the heel of a sourdough loaf, charred along its upper ridge where the red coils of the toaster caught it. I press a thick slab of unsalted butter against the warm crumb. The fat stays stubbornly solid. I lift the crust to my mouth and bite.
　Black carbon scrapes my palate; right behind it comes the dense, tallowy chill of the dairy. My tongue searches the dark cavern behind my teeth for the words that belong to this morning—the modifier for the sour tang of fermented dough, the exact epithet for butter that has kept the chill of the larder. Yesterday they waited in neat rows like silver spoons in a velvet drawer. Today the velvet is bare. Grease coats my gums; ash settles on my tongue; the crunch of my own jawbone echoes loud inside my skull. Under the table, I rub the pad of my right thumb against my index finger. Shhh-shhh. Dry skin rasping against dry skin.

[M1-C1-E01-S002]
　Across the floorboards stands the oak lectern bearing an early volume of the Oxford English Dictionary, open at the letter A. Long seasons of my life were spent inside those triple columns, trimming dead branches from Latin roots, tracing how an Anglo-Saxon grunt softened into a vowel fit for a vicarage parlour.
　I lean over the tissue-thin India paper and run my fingernail down the entry I drafted in a colder autumn: aphasia, from the Greek ἀφασία, speechlessness. Below the headword, historical quotations march in chronological ranks. Yet as I stare at the italicised qualifiers—chronic, amnesic, nominal, transient—their tiny serifs begin to twitch and loosen like legs shed by drowned spiders. Because the core of the things they describe has begun to rot in my left temporal lobe, the ornaments can no longer hold on. I shut the heavy buckram covers with both hands. A puff of dry paper-dust rises against my chin, smelling of vanilla and old hide glue.

Episode 02: Tape on the Mahogany

[M1-C1-E02-S003]
　By noon I have bought rolls of painters' masking tape from the ironmonger on Garnet Street. I sit at the mahogany table, turning a Blackwing pencil inside a brass wedge sharpener. A ribbon of cedar curls over my knuckles; I lift the shaving to my nostrils and inhale the resinous wood before biting the painted barrel between my molars, feeling the soft basswood yield to the enamel.
　I tear a strip of tape, press it flat against the ceramic cylinder holding my cold Earl Grey, and print in block capitals: MUG. I cross the worn rug and label the cast-iron ribs below the sill: RADIATOR. I crouch by the floorboards: OAK. Soon the flat resembles a room catalogued by a frantic bailiff. Every solid object wears a white paper bandage bearing its proper designation. So long as graphite binds the syllable to the timber, the furniture cannot slip through the walls while my back is turned.

[M1-C1-E02-S004]
　A pale strip of tape dangles from my left thumb. I stand before the river window, pencil poised, trying to anchor the quality of the afternoon light as it filters through the Thames mist. Bleak. Wistful. Bilious. Lead-coloured.
　The graphite point hovers over the crêpe paper, trembling. Where does a man stick the label for bleak? Press it to the pane, and it names the glass. Press it to the dampness, and it names the condensation. Qualifiers have no skin of their own, no edges to take the gum. I roll the sticky strip into a tight pellet between my palms and drop it into my cardigan pocket. Beyond the brick sill, a vast, nameless weight leans against the warehouse glass.


Chapter 2　The Cello Case

Episode 03: Elena's Dust

[M1-C2-E03-S005]
　In the alcove beside the chimney breast stands the fibreglass cello case Elena left behind when she carried the rest of her life north to Hampstead. She had bought a lighter carbon shell for an autumn tour; this scarred black cocoon stayed in the corner like an upright sarcophagus.
　I kneel on the boards and draw my forefinger across its curved belly. Grey dust gathers in a felt-like roll under my nail. Beneath the dust, the lacquer still breathes a faint ghost of dark pine rosin. When the first nouns began to slip through the sieve of my brain—when I called her music stand a "metal stork" during rehearsal and could not summon the word arpeggio—terror made me vicious. "Your cello is horsehair scraping dried sheep-gut," I told her, sneering over my teacup so she would not notice my fingers rattling against the saucer. Better that she despise me for a cold-blooded pedant than stay to watch the lexicographer drool over his alphabet. Now I try to summon the colour of her coat on the afternoon the front door latched behind her, and find only grey static.

[M1-C2-E03-S006]
　I leave the lid shut. Inside lies only crushed blue plush and the velvet hollow where a maple bridge once pressed. Instead, I hook my thumbnail beneath the lower brass latch and flick it upward.
　Clack.
　A sharp metallic report snaps through the room, followed by a low, cavernous hum from the empty shell—a deep G vibrating through the soles of my wool socks. Vibration requires no grammar. It asks for no adjective. I flick the brass tongue again, pressing my cheek against the cold fibreglass until the ringing merges with the diesel throb of a tugboat passing outside.

Episode 04: The Niece with the Code

[M1-C2-E04-S007]
　Toward dusk the entry buzzer rasps. Mayuki climbs the stairs from the street, bringing the cold smell of Shoreditch rain and a brown paper bag heavy with dark purple figs. While the kettle builds to a rumble on the hob, she sets her silver laptop among my white tape bandages, her dark eyes moving from KETTLE to TAP to SPOON without a flicker of pity.
　I pinch dry Earl Grey leaves from the caddy and chew them raw between my molars. Bitter bergamot oil stings my gums, slicing through the wool in my head.
　"When I was small, Uncle Julian," Mayuki says, lifting her screen, "I cried in the garden because I couldn't tell the difference between shadow and shade. You drew a cedar tree on a napkin and showed me how words hold the light at different angles." She swivels the monitor toward my chair. Across a dark ground, lines of pale serif text float inside nested brackets of code. "The neurologist says the semantic net unravels from the specific to the general. So don't fight the unravelling on paper. Give the words to me. Let me write a page that forgets at the same pace you do."

[M1-C2-E04-S008]
　Warm exhaust from her processor fan brushes my wrist. I peer at the glowing Document Object Model in her inspector pane: spans and classes nested like wooden dolls, each word wrapped in its grammatical shroud.
　I reach out with two fingers and tap her black plastic keys. C-A-L-I-G-O. Latin for mist, darkness, vertigo. Then: L-A-C-U-N-A. A missing line in an ancient manuscript.
　"A dictionary in reverse," I murmur, rubbing my thumb against my forefinger. Shhh-shhh. "As the reader moves down the glass, the modifiers bleach into the dark. When they touch a name, it drops through the floorboards."
　Mayuki's fingers dance across the trackpad, binding an event listener to my voice. "A monument built of tide-sand," she whispers. "We'll let the browser keep the ghosts."


Act II — The Fracture of Nouns


Chapter 3　The Kitchen of Nameless Things

Episode 05: The Pear That Was Not a Pear

[M2-C3-E05-S009]
　February frost rims the kitchen casement. On the counter sits an earthenware bowl holding the fruit Mayuki poached in white wine and cinnamon bark last night. I lift one by its shrivelled stem. Dark syrup drips onto my knuckle, thick and cold.
　I know where this object grows—on twisted grey branches leaning over warm Mediterranean stones. I know that splitting its purple skin reveals a wet crimson hollow packed with tiny seeds. Yet when I reach into the cupboard of my skull for its designation, my hand closes on air.
　"Pear," I say aloud to the tiles. Wrong. A pear is pale, gritty, swollen at the base. This is smaller, slack-skinned. "Plum." Wrong. A plum hides a single hard stone at the centre. "Fruit," I mutter, and even that broader word hangs loose around the object like a dead man's coat. Finally: "This sweet thing."
　I push the whole dark globe into my mouth. The skin yields; spiced wine floods my palate; the seeds crackle between my teeth like river gravel. With its name gone, nothing stands between my tongue and the pulp. The sweetness is almost violent.

[M2-C3-E05-S010]
　Mayuki sits opposite me on a Saturday morning, unpacking a canvas bag onto the dark wood table. She arranges a neat row of household things: a perforated metal basin for draining boiled greens, a wire mesh scoop for catching tea leaves, a twisted steel worm for drawing corks, and a lemon.
　"Take your time, Julian," she says, keeping her voice level, her fingertips resting on the edge of her keyboard.
　I point at the yellow oval. "Lemon," I say.
　I shift my finger to the metal basin punched with small star-shaped holes. Behind my eyes I see boiling water rushing through it, white steam clouding my spectacles. I mime shaking two side-handles in the air. "The... the water-sieve. The basin that lets the wet out."
　Mayuki does not offer colander. She knows how fiercely I loathe the alms of a rescued noun. Instead, she depresses a key. On her dark screen, `<span class="noun" data-word="colander">vessel</span>` shivers once and collapses into a leaden slot: `[____]`. My throat tightens; I swallow dry spit and point to the wire tea-scoop in silence.

Episode 06: Bread and Knife

[M2-C3-E06-S011]
　The loaf on the chopping board has gone stale, its crust baked into brown armour. I pull open the drawer and close my fist around the riveted wooden grip of the long steel blade with the scalloped teeth.
　As I lift it into the pale light from the casement, the domestic compound bread-knife dissolves. What my right fist holds is stripped of its tame kitchen category. Long Sheffield steel gleams in the gloom, ground into a row of Ripper fangs, the heavy tang pulling at the tendons of my wrist. I stare at the polished flat of the metal, watching my own warped eye stare back. When the lexicon dies, objects do not grow blurry; they grow feral. Every tool in the room bristles with raw iron and edge.

[M2-C3-E06-S012]
　I set the serrated teeth against the loaf and saw downward. The steel skids off the hard flour-dusted dome and grazes the knuckle of my left thumb.
　A shallow parting of the skin. A bead of bright carmine swells from the crease, round and heavy as mercury.
　"Red," I whisper. The syllable holds fast.
　A hot wire of pain climbs the nerve from thumb to elbow.
　"Hurt," I say. That word holds too. I press my knuckle to my lips and taste the hot copper of my own salt. Pain sits at the bottom of the well where the water never freezes.


Chapter 4　The Rain Without a Sky

Episode 07: River Thames in Grey

[M2-C4-E07-S013]
　Low water at Wapping. Most of the white tape bandages have curled and dropped from the furniture like shed skin; I leave them on the rug because the block capitals written on their backs—LINTEL, RADIATOR, SKIRTING—now resemble Phoenician scratches made by a dead tenant.
　I stand by the tall glass and look down. The proper name Thames has vanished. The common noun river has followed it. Beneath the brick wall lies a vast open trench of wet, breathing grease. Long banks of black silt glisten like the backs of whales stranded in the drizzle, rising with the stench of rotted bladderwrack, diesel oil, and centuries of rusted chain. A colossal muscle of grey mud heaves seaward under the rain.

[M2-C4-E07-S014]
　Along the narrow cobbles on the landward side of the warehouse, figures move through the dusk rain. Half a year ago I would have parsed them like a sentence: solicitors hastening toward the station, a courier hunched over handlebars, a woman leading a brindle terrier.
　Now the nouns that sorted them into professions and errands have washed into the gutter. I watch two-legged warm bodies moving beneath bobbing domes of taut black cloth. I watch rubber soles kicking silver arcs from puddles. Stripped of their titles, they look piercingly fragile—soft creatures carrying paper parcels of food through the falling water, leaning into the wind so as not to fall.

Episode 08: The Lost Subjunctive

[M2-C4-E08-S015]
　On my desk lies a buff folder of offprints: my monograph on the subjunctive mood in Middle English lyrics. I open the paper wrapper near the middle.
　"Were the speaker to posit a counterfactual desire, the inflectional ending would..."
　My eyes skid across the print as though the ink were smeared with lard. If. Were. Would have been. Whomsoever. The small brass hinges of grammar—the pins that allow a human creature to throw a bridge into yesterday or tomorrow, or into a room that never existed—have rusted through. A thought can no longer turn a corner. Every hypothetical door is bricked up. Only the table in front of my nose remains.

[M2-C4-E08-S016]
　From the bottom drawer I pull the blue airmail pad filled with unsent drafts to Elena. Every sheet begins with a conditional clause: If I had told you the diagnosis in October... Had I not mocked your Brahms... Would that we might...
　I can no longer assemble the machinery of my own remorse. Regret demands a parallel world, a grammatical mood in which the latch did not click shut. Lose the subjunctive, and you lose the syntax required to haunt yourself.
　I gather the blue sheets together, grip the top margin in both fists, and rip them down the spine. Then across. Rrrr-ip. Blue flakes snow into the wicker basket. I am walled inside the present indicative, and the room is extraordinarily quiet.


Act III — The Bare Verbs & The Void


Chapter 5　The Hand on the Glass

Episode 09: Touch, Cold, Hold

[M3-C5-E09-S017]
　Spring.
　No month. No label.
　Stand before the tall clear wall.
　Lift the hand. Press the flat of the skin against the hard cold.
　Cold bites the palm. Breath goes out of the mouth and blooms white on the clear wall.
　Drag a finger through the white mist. Open a clear streak. Through the streak, the wet bright outside shines.
　Rub thumb against finger. Shhh-shhh.
　Press. Stay. Breathe.

[M3-C5-E09-S018]
　The door opens. Steps on the wood.
　She comes in. Dark hair. Small hands. Smells of rain and bitter leaf-steam.
　No word for her. Only Her. Only Warm.
　She stands close. She looks at my chest. The wool coat on my shoulders hangs crooked—low button pushed into the high slit.
　Her fingers rise. She slips the round bone disc out, guides it through the right hole, smooths the rough wool over my collarbone.
　Her palm rests on my shoulder. Heartbeat in her wrist.
　Warm. Hold. Safe.

Episode 10: The Code That Breaks

[M3-C5-E10-S019]
　Sit side by side.
　The flat light-box rests open on her knees.
　Inside the dark square, pale marks drift like ash in water.
　She touches the smooth pad. One mark goes dark. Another mark blurs into smoke.
　In the corner of the glass, small figures tick down: 3%... 2%... 1%...
　All the heavy stones I carried inside my skull—they live in her box now. Drifting. Sinking. Turning into still water.
　She turns her face to me. Wetness shines along her lower lashes.
　I tap the rim of the light-box with my knuckle and nod once. Let them go.

[M3-C5-E10-S020]
　Reach into the deep pocket of the wool coat.
　Fingers find the short stick of wood. Tooth-marks dented all along the paint. Smells of forest resin and black stone.
　Draw it out. Nothing left to name.
　Take her small hand. Turn the palm up.
　Lay the bitten wood across the lines of her skin. Fold her fingers over it.
　Keep.


Chapter 6　The Light Itself

Episode 11: The Last Hundred Letters

[M3-C6-E11-S021]
　Turn toward the tall clear wall.
　The grey outside splits wide.
　Yellow pours through.
　It spills across the rough red wall. It floods the wood floor. It climbs my knees, my ribs, my throat.
　Once, a coin sat over my eye. Sun. Afternoon. April. Gold.
　The coin has fallen away.
　Raw yellow heat strikes the naked eye with nothing between. Every speck of dust turning in the air blazes like a world. The rough wall burns gold.
　Shut the lids. Red-gold glows through the blood of the skin.
　Open.

[M3-C6-E11-S022]
　Look.
　Taste.
　Shine.
　Breathe.
　In.
　Out.
　Stay.
　Be.
